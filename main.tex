\title{Irrationality of the trilogarithm at reciprocals of large integers}
\author{Ruiyi Zhang}
\address{Independent Researcher, Columbia, Maryland, USA}
\email{ryzfsu@gmail.com}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 11J72; Secondary 33B30}
\keywords{trilogarithm, irrationality, determinant, logarithmic energy}

\begin{abstract}
We prove that $\operatorname{Li}_3(1/a)$ is irrational for every integer
$a\ge67$ and every integer $a\le-70$. Earlier ranges are $|a|\ge1038$ (Hata), $a\ge1173$
(Panzone), and $a\ge486$, $a\le-471$ (Marcovecchio). For $4\mid m$ we form
an $m\times m$ determinant. Its entries are linear forms in $1$ and
$\operatorname{Li}_3(1/a)$. The
kernel has only triple poles, so no other polylogarithm enters. We bound a
common denominator of the coefficients. The determinant does not vanish along a
prime-power subsequence that depends on $a$. A logarithmic energy bounds
its size. Exact density tables with directed rounding give the
energy bounds at $a=67$ and $a=-70$. A slope comparison extends them to all
$a\ge67$ and all $a\le-70$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For $|z|<1$, define
\[
\operatorname{Li}_3(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{z^n}{n^3}.
\]

Hata \cite{Hata} constructed explicit rational approximations to
polylogarithms at $1/q$. By Marcovecchio \cite[p.~229]{Marcovecchio},
Hata's method \cite[Corollary~2.2]{Hata} gives the linear independence
over $\mathbb Q$ of
\[
1,\ \operatorname{Li}_1(1/a),\ \operatorname{Li}_2(1/a),\
\operatorname{Li}_3(1/a)
\]
for every integer $a$ with $|a|\ge1038$. Panzone
\cite[Theorem~2, p.~108]{Panzone} gave a short proof that
$\operatorname{Li}_3(1/d)$ is irrational for every integer $d\ge1173$. He
attributes this result to Hata. Marcovecchio
\cite[Theorem~7.1, p.~228; p.~229]{Marcovecchio} extended the linear
independence to $a\ge486$ and $a\le-471$. In particular
$\operatorname{Li}_3(1/a)$ is irrational in these ranges.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every integer $a\ge67$ and every integer $a\le-70$,
$\operatorname{Li}_3(1/a)$ is irrational.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} concerns $\operatorname{Li}_3(1/a)$ alone. In their
ranges, the results of Hata and Marcovecchio give the stronger linear
independence of four numbers.

The method is the determinant method of \cite{Zh} for the dilogarithm,
carried to weight three. We use only the determinant criterion of
\cite[Section~3]{Zh}; Fact~\ref{fact:criterion} restates it with proof.
The analytic estimate uses Andreief's identity~\cite{Andreief}, Cauchy's
double alternant~\cite{Krattenthaler}, logarithmic
energy~\cite{SaffTotik}, and the prime number theorem~\cite{Davenport}.

Let $m$ be a positive multiple of $4$. Let $G_{m,a}(T)$ be the determinant polynomial of
Section~\ref{sec:determinant}. Section~\ref{sec:integerization} gives a
common denominator with limiting logarithmic rate $\tau(a)$, where
\[
S=\frac{643}{96}+4\log2-\frac{25}{8}\log5,
\qquad
\tau(a)=S+\frac9{16}\log|a-1|-\frac18\log|a|.
\]
Section~\ref{sec:nonvanishing} proves nonvanishing along $m=4p^k$, with one
prime $p$ for each $a$. Section~\ref{sec:analytic} bounds the determinant
by the energy function $A$. Section~\ref{sec:energy} proves
\[
\begin{gathered}
A(\log67)\ge6.2732861583,\qquad \tau(67)<6.272107,\\
A(\log70)\ge6.3100053850,\qquad \tau(-70)<6.307708,\\
\max\{\tau(67)-A(\log67),\tau(-70)-A(\log70)\}<0.
\end{gathered}
\]
These bounds give Theorem~\ref{thm:main} for $a=67$ and $a=-70$.
Section~\ref{sec:rays} compares slopes in $\log|a|$ and extends the
theorem to the full ranges.

The proof uses a computer at three points. The energy bounds of
Section~\ref{sec:energy} use exact rational and directed interval
arithmetic. They act on the density tables printed in
Appendix~\ref{app:cert}. The bounds for
$\tau(67)$ and $\tau(-70)$ use exact rational series. The seed identity of
Section~\ref{sec:nonvanishing} is an exact polynomial computation.
Appendix~\ref{app:seed} prints its coefficients. Appendix~\ref{app:checks}
lists these and the other exact checks. The use of AI tools is described
at the end of the paper.

\section{Computer checks}\label{app:checks}

Table~\ref{tab:checks-clean} lists the scripts. They do the exact and
directed-interval computations of the paper. They and
their outputs accompany the paper as ancillary files. They run under Python~3.12.3. \texttt{cert.py} and
\texttt{check\_energy.py} need python-flint~0.9.0, which provides the Arb
interval arithmetic; the other scripts use only the standard library. The
prime-power row checks the pole parts at $p=13$. This prime is admissible
at $a=67$ and $a=-70$. It is not the prime chosen in
Section~\ref{sec:nonvanishing}. Lemma~\ref{lem:one-carry} proves the pole
finite parts. The proof uses neither of these two rows; they are diagnostic
checks.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:energy}, \texttt{cert.py} computes the
certificate with 256 cells and 256 subcells per cell. The script
\texttt{check\_energy.py} reads the tables of Appendix~\ref{app:cert} from
the source of this paper. It then recomputes the bounds with the routine of
\texttt{cert.py}. The subcells have length $1/(128\cdot256)$. On each subcell
it splits $\Psi$ into the convex part $\Psi_1$ and the concave part $\Psi_2$
of that proof. It evaluates $\Psi_2(x_0)$, $\Psi_2(x_1)$, $\Psi_1(\bar x)$ and
$\Psi_1'(\bar x)$ as Arb balls. These give the two endpoint values of the affine minorant
$\underline\Psi$ as balls. It takes the upper endpoints of
$\Lambda-\underline\Psi(x_0)$ and $\Lambda-\underline\Psi(x_1)$. The
positive part of an affine function has an exact integral in terms of its
two endpoint values. This integral increases with them. The upper endpoints of
these subcell contributions are summed, so the result bounds the
positive-part integral from above.
